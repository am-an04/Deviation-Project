# MVP Severity Assessment Guidance: Severity Classification Guidelines

> **Notice**: This document represents internal MVP Severity Assessment Guidance for decision support only. It does not represent or claim to be an official FDA, EMA, GMP, or ICH regulatory standard.

## 1. Overview and Intent
This guidance defines criteria for categorizing deviation events into four standardized decision-support severity levels: Minor, Moderate, Major, and Critical. It assists Quality professionals in consistently categorizing deviations during intake.

---

## 2. Severity Classification Levels

### MINOR SEVERITY
- **Definition**: A limited deviation with low potential impact, where no product quality, sterility, or efficacy impact is identified, and the event is effectively contained.
- **Criteria**:
  - Operating parameter was slightly out of typical operating range but remained within validated/proven acceptable limits.
  - Brief duration excursion where product contact was isolated or unaffected.
  - Minor typographical or clerical correction not altering operational decisions or product release.
  - Standard containment measures are immediately effective with no residual risk.
- **Examples**:
  - Minor temperature excursion in a non-critical ambient storage buffer for less than 15 minutes within validated safety margins.
  - Packaging barcode scan retry during secondary labeling with 100% verification intact.

---

### MODERATE SEVERITY
- **Definition**: A meaningful process, equipment, or documentation deviation requiring investigation to confirm product quality and process consistency, where containment or review is in place.
- **Criteria**:
  - Parameter deviation outside validated ranges that was promptly halted and placed on quarantine/hold.
  - Exceeded approved duration or mixing/blending times where downstream quality requires laboratory verification or in-process testing.
  - Equipment alarm triggered requiring minor corrective maintenance before resuming operations.
  - No confirmed direct product defect or patient safety risk at the time of initial logging, but potential impact cannot be ruled out without investigation.
- **Examples**:
  - Blending time exceeded approved 45 minutes by 17 minutes; batch placed on quarantine hold; blend uniformity testing required.
  - Sensor calibration discrepancy detected during shift review without evidence of compromised batch limits.

---

### MAJOR SEVERITY
- **Definition**: A significant deviation with substantial potential impact to product quality, intermediate specification, or process control, or a broad compliance concern.
- **Criteria**:
  - Parameter excursion exceeding critical process parameters (CPP) or critical quality attributes (CQA).
  - Potential contamination risk, environmental monitoring excursion in Grade B or Grade C cleanrooms, or post-filtration filter integrity test decay exceeding limits.
  - Multi-batch exposure or systemic equipment malfunction affecting finished or in-process product.
  - Physical defect or foreign matter observed in production streams.
- **Examples**:
  - Sterilizing filter pressure decay rate exceeded 0.30 bar/min post-filtration; holding tank placed on hold; integrity failure potential.
  - Bioreactor dissolved oxygen drop below specification for 3 hours during peak growth phase.

---

### CRITICAL SEVERITY
- **Definition**: A deviation presenting a potential serious impact to patient safety, sterility assurance compromise, serious product quality degradation, or critical compliance failure.
- **Criteria**:
  - Confirmed non-sterility, positive media fill failure, or direct breach in Grade A aseptic processing zone.
  - Potent drug cross-contamination or gross mix-up of active pharmaceutical ingredients.
  - Known adverse patient safety risk or distributed defective product reaching market channels.
- **Examples**:
  - Particulate contamination detected in injectable sterile fill line during vial filling.
  - Direct operator contamination event within sterile filling needle zone during active filling.
